# R2b The lobby's cast: design

Design addendum, 2026-10-12, to `2026-10-10-hammer-time-rebuild-design.md` (the binding spec;
this file replaces its §7.1 for the four lobby roles and fills in §16 R2's "four lobby NPC rigs
with behaviors"). Agreed with the owner in the R2b brainstorm the same day. Where this file and
the main spec differ, this file wins for the lobby's cast.

## 1. The brief and the decisions

The owner's direction: "Don't just rehash the old cast. You have enhanced graphic hardware
capability. Be creative and make it look good. The whole point of a rebuild is to do better."
And on difficulty: "make it fun and challenging but not impossible."

Decisions the owner made in the brainstorm:

1. **The cast is painted like Hammer.** Each person is a full-body painting in Hammer's art style,
   generated by the owner from prompts written here, copying Hammer's own body image for pose and
   framing ("pose-locked"), then cut into layers and skinned over a skeleton the way his body is.
   Not the shipped photos, not code-drawn figures.
2. **Two different people per role**: eight paintings. Repeats within a role are recolored and
   resized in code.
3. **A slapstick ensemble**: each role has one signature attack with a big readable wind-up and one
   signature way of going down, and their gags set each other off (runaway carts, sliding
   bellhops, flying bodies, spilled water and coffee, paper planes).
4. The fight rules (§3), the placement (§5, left to my judgment within "fun and challenging but not
   impossible"), the art and animation plan (§6, §7) and the split and testing (§10, §11) as
   presented.

Decisions I took for the owner (report these):

1. Knocking out staff does **not** charge the Super Smash: three bags of nuts stay its only fuel
   (the owner's one-job rule of 2026-10-12).
2. When he loses a life, the cast comes back as the run placed it, like the pickups and crates.
3. At most two cast members attack him at once (§4).
4. Students are out of melee: they duck the mallet and sidestep stomps. The lecture and snot are
   the answers to them.
5. The props the cast holds (tray, glasses, coffee cup, phone, paper planes, the cart) are drawn
   in code so they can move, fly and break; the bellhops' pillbox hat is a painted image so it can
   fly off.
6. Until the paintings arrive the cast draws as code-drawn stand-ins on the same skeleton, and
   those stay as the fallback when art is missing (`&noart=1`).
7. The staff stash stays without cast.

## 2. The cast

Heights are soles to crown at zoom 1 (Hammer stands about 330). Speeds and times are starting
values for the feel pass; the shipped rules (`src/game/engine.ts`, shipped units about a sixth of
these) are the source of the spirit, not the numbers.

### 2.1 The bellhop (two hits, his cart is his weapon)

People: **bellhop-1**, an older, upright gentleman, proud of his job (355 tall); **bellhop-2**, a
young woman, eager to please (315 tall). Red jacket with gold buttons and braid, black trousers
with a gold stripe, white gloves, the red pillbox hat (a separate image, so it can fly off).

- **Idle**: stands behind his brass luggage cart buffing the rail with a cloth, rocks on his heels,
  whistles.
- **Notice** (Hammer within 900 px on his level, either side; he turns to him): a double-take (head to Hammer, back,
  back again), "Welcome to the Marriott!" or another line.
- **Attack, the runaway cart**: a 0.7 s wind-up (he grips the cart, crouches and rocks it back,
  the wheels squeal, the ! burst), then a shove: the cart rolls at Hammer at 650 px/s and slows
  (friction 260 px/s², which replaces the carts' 900 everywhere, so the spare cart rolls far too). A rolling cart (faster than 150 px/s) costs a pip on contact. He follows
  it at a walk, collects it where it stops and wheels it back to his spot; then he can shove
  again (3 s rest). A second-hit bellhop who got back up winds up in 0.55 s.
- **Answers**: jump the cart (from the floor it is a full jump; from the desk or a sofa it is easy),
  or bonk it back: a bonked cart reverses at 1100 px/s and bowls over whoever it meets,
  him included ("return to sender").
- **Going down**: hit one (mallet, snot, stomp, a puddle) knocks his hat flying (a spinning prop
  that lands and stays until he walks off) and drops him on his backside, dazed with circling stars,
  for 4 s; then he gets up and comes back at you. Hit two while he is down sends him sliding on his
  back along the marble at 1100 px/s (friction 700), bowling over anyone in his path (the chain,
  §3.5); when the slide stops he is out.

### 2.2 The server (warns, then rushes; one hit)

People: **server-1**, a young woman with her dark hair tied back (320 tall); **server-2**, a lanky
young man with curly hair (360 tall). White shirt, black vest, long black apron, black trousers.
She carries a round tray of water glasses at shoulder height in her near hand.

- **Idle**: polishes a glass from the tray, scans the room, patrols a stretch at 200 px/s.
- **Notice** (within 680 px on her level, either side): brightens, "Can I start you off with some
  waters?!"
- **Attack, the rush**: a 0.45 s warning (she drops into a sprinter's crouch with the tray held high,
  the ! burst, a sharp "order up" ding), then a rush at 420 px/s for up to 2 s or 1000 px. Contact
  while rushing costs a pip. A rush ends in a skid (0.4 s, "Be right with you!") and a 1.5 s rest
  before she can notice again.
- **Answers**: jump or stomp her, bonk her, snot her, or leave a puddle in her path. In the
  conference wing, hop onto a table.
- **Going down** (one hit): the tray flips, the glasses spin up in arcs and shatter on the marble
  (shards with glints, a tinkle), ice skitters along the floor, she spins and lands flat; the spill
  leaves a wet floor (a puddle 260 px wide for 8 s) that trips the next staffer. Dazed 2.5 s, then
  she gets up: "I'm going on break." and walks off.

### 2.3 The office worker (a walking hazard; one hit)

People: **office-1**, a balding man in his forties with glasses (345 tall); **office-2**, a woman
in her thirties with a sharp bob (325 tall). Business casual (shirt and loosened tie, or blazer),
a lanyard, white earbuds. Coffee cup in the near hand, phone in the far hand, face lit by it.

- **Patrol, head-down**: he walks a stretch at 160 px/s staring at his phone and never looks up.
  The phone's glow, his steady approach and a phone chime when he comes within 400 px are the tell;
  at that pace he needs over two seconds to cross a screen. Meeting him head-on while he marches
  costs a pip: the coffee goes all over Hammer (brown droplets, never over his face) and "Oh. Were
  you standing there?"
- **The projector ambush** (office worker 2 in the conference wing): he waits behind the projector
  screen; the projector's beam throws his silhouette onto the lit screen, so the player sees him.
  When Hammer comes within 500 px, the silhouette rears back (0.5 s, "Got a minute?"), he leaps
  out and flings his coffee: a hot arc (700 px/s, gravity) that costs a pip on contact and leaves a
  coffee puddle. Then he marches like office worker 1.
- **Lecture**: he freezes mid-sentence, "Per my last em—".
- **Going down** (one hit): the cup flies up and the coffee splashes in an arc and a puddle (150 px,
  7 s), he folds like a lawn chair; dazed 2.5 s; "I'm putting this in an email." and he walks off.
- **The shaft**: knocked flying over the open elevator shaft, he drops in with a fading "Per my last
  emaaaail": OUT OF ORDER, 500 points.

### 2.4 The student (out of melee; paper planes)

People: **student-1**, curly dark hair, round glasses, an olive overshirt, jeans and a maroon
backpack (315 tall); **student-2**, a grey hoodie, a beanie, joggers and a black backpack
(335 tall). White earbuds; a phone held up, filming, with a red REC dot.

- **Idle**: leans on the mezzanine railing on both elbows, scrolling; or sits cross-legged on a
  banquet table.
- **Notice** (Hammer within 1100 px, on the floor below or on the balcony): phone up, filming,
  "I'm rating your customer service." or another line.
- **Attack, the paper plane**: a 0.65 s wind-up (folds a plane, cocks the arm, "Heads up!", the !
  burst), then a throw: a paper plane on a real glide path (launch at 520 px/s, a lift and drag
  model that dips, stalls and drifts, with banking and spin), aimed where Hammer is at the throw;
  from the railing it glides down to the floor route, on the balcony it flies along it. About 1.5 s
  of flight: easy to step out of the way. A plane costs a pip on contact. Each student rests 4 to
  5.8 s between throws (seeded); no more than three planes in the air.
- **Answers**: step aside, crouch, jump, or swat it with the mallet (SWAT!, the plane shreds into
  confetti, 50 points).
- **Out of melee**: when Hammer comes within 300 px on the balcony a student hops back if there is
  room; a mallet swing makes them duck under it ("lol"); a stomp makes them sidestep.
- **Lecture**: schooled for 9 s: they slump to the floor, arms crossed, a grey scribble cloud,
  a retort ("Okay, boomer."), no planes. 150 points.
- **Snot**: "EW!": they run off gagging, out for good (100 points).
- **The shield, the smash, a flying body, a cart**: they tumble like staff, then run off.

### 2.5 Everyone

- **The double-take** on first noticing him, once per life.
- **Idle life**: each role's habits above, plus breathing, weight shifts and looking around.
- **Knocked out**: dazed with stars, then up woozy, a line, and they walk away from Hammer and are
  gone once off camera. Nobody stays as a body. Out cast never hurts and can't be hit (the downed
  bellhop is the one exception: his second hit).
- **Written lines** in speech bubbles: the shipped `STAFF_LINES`, `STAFF_SIGHS`, `RETORTS` and
  `STUDENT_EXCHANGES` replies, plus new ones for the new beats (the lines quoted in this file and a
  few more per role). Never new recordings.

## 3. The rules of a fight

### 3.1 What hurts him

Only an attack under way costs a patience pip: a rushing server, a rolling cart, a marching office
worker and his flung coffee, a paper plane. Each goes through `world.hit(fromX)` (the existing
damage: a pip, the hurt or the knockdown, 1.45 s of grace). Walking into anyone who is not attacking
bumps him to a stop with a little push and a line ("Sir, check-in is at four."), no pip.

### 3.2 What knocks them down

| Hammer's move | Staff | Students |
|---|---|---|
| Mallet (the swing's swept path) | down | duck it ("lol") |
| Snot (hot snot goes through one) | down, slimed | "EW!", run off |
| Stomp (landing on them from above; he bounces) | down | sidestep |
| Lecture (the `lecture` event's `reach`) | frozen 1.8 s | schooled 9 s |
| Super Smash burst | flying | flying, then run off |
| Buc-ee's shield (running into them) | flying | flying, then run off |
| A puddle (snot, water, coffee) | slip: feet out, flat, stars | no effect |
| A rolling cart, a sliding bellhop, a flying body | down (chain) | flying, then run off |

"Down" is a knockout for the server and the office worker and the first hit for the bellhop. A
frozen staffer drops any wind-up. A slip shortens the puddle to 1.2 s.

### 3.3 Points

A knockdown 100; a slip 150; a schooled student 150; a frozen staffer 50; a swatted plane 50; a
shield knockdown 200; a student run off by snot 100; the shaft 500; the chain (§3.5).

### 3.4 Recordings and thoughts

When he bests staff, `AudioBus.taunt("staff")` (the shipped chance 0.35 and 3.5 s gap: "They need
to get fired.", "They can't take feedback.", "They get all butt hurt."); when he schools or snots a
student, `taunt("student")` ("They got a problem.", "Go grow some man balls."). When no recording
plays, sometimes (0.45) one of his written comebacks (`STAFF_COMEBACKS`, the bellhop using the
`hotel` set) as a thought bubble. The lecture's comic word is SCHOOLED! when it schools anyone,
otherwise LISTEN UP!.

### 3.5 The chain

A rolling cart (faster than 150 px/s), a sliding bellhop and a body flying from a knock each carry
a chain: every staffer it knocks down scores the shipped `CHAIN` (100, 200, 400, 800, 1000, 2000,
4000, 8000) by its count, and the eighth is a life. The chain belongs to the thing moving, so two
carts keep two counts.

### 3.6 Hammer and the cast's leftovers

Puddles trip staff, never Hammer. Glass shards, ice, coffee and confetti are effects only. A flying
cast member never hurts Hammer.

## 4. Fairness and difficulty

- Every wind-up lasts at least 0.45 s and shows a ! burst over the attacker's head with a sound;
  the office worker's march is the one attack without a wind-up, and it is slow and audible (§2.3).
- **Two attackers at most**: a director hands out attack tickets; a cast member needs one to start a
  wind-up, and at most two are out at once. A ticket is returned when the attack ends (a rolling
  cart holds its bellhop's ticket until it stops). The office worker's march takes no ticket.
- After a hit he has his 1.45 s of grace; attacks pass through him in it.
- The target: a careful player can clear the lobby untouched; a first run costs a few pips and maybe
  a life; nowhere is a trap. The scripted player (§11) wins both routes against the live cast.
- Everything reads on a phone: the ! burst is at least a fifth of Hammer's height, the planes are at
  least 70 px long, and every telegraph has a sound.

## 6. The art

### 6.1 What the owner generates

`art/outgoing/batch-3-cast.txt` (rewritten for this design) is the brief, in the batch-1 format:

1. **A test image first**: `bellhop-1.png`. I check that it came back in Hammer's pose and cuts
   cleanly before the rest are made; the prompt is fixed if it did not.
2. **The other seven bodies**: `bellhop-2`, `server-1`, `server-2`, `office-1`, `office-2`,
   `student-1`, `student-2`.
3. **`bellhop-hat.png`**: the red pillbox hat on its own, side view.
4. **Optional, later**: one face edit per person, `<person>-silly.png` (the body image edited with
   only the face changed: eyes crossed, mouth slack), used while dazed.

Every body attaches Hammer's `art/incoming/hammer/body-affliction.png` as the pose, framing and
style to copy (strict left profile, both arms forward and down about 30 degrees with open empty
hands and a gap at the belly, feet flat with the same small stagger), plus a portrait for the style;
each person keeps their own build and height. Hands empty, no props, the bellhops bareheaded.
Files land in `art/incoming/cast/`, hashed into `art/manifest.json` like Hammer's.

### 6.2 Cutting and rigging

- The art pipeline (`scripts/art-cut.mjs`, `art-sync.mjs`, `src/render/skin.ts`) is generalized from
  Hammer's bodies to any body: one cut file per person, `art/cuts/cast-<person>.json`, with the
  painted joints and six layer outlines (Hammer's five plus a head layer, so heads can turn and
  double-take), measured with `art-grid.mjs`. Pose-locking means Hammer's joints and outlines are
  the starting point for each.
- The skeleton is generalized: Hammer's rest table stays his; each person gets a rest skeleton from
  their painting's joints, with the same joint names, so every clip works on every body.
- Heads are scaled toward Hammer's caricature proportions (his is 0.26 of his height); the scale per
  person is chosen on a board with the owner.
- Recolors shift only the uniform's and the hair's hues, from masks recorded in the cut file.
- The face edits, when present, swap in whole for the head layer while dazed.
- Rim light: the side of each cast member facing the nearest lobby lamp gets a thin warm lit edge.

### 6.3 Stand-ins and fallback

Until a person's painting is cut, they draw as a code-drawn figure in the style of the
brainstorm's board panel C (inked outline, three-tone fills, the role's uniform), built from the
same solved skeleton, with every clip. The stand-in stays as the fallback when the art is missing
or fails to load.

## 7. Animation and effects

- **Clips per role** (rotations over the shared joint names, `src/sim/rig`): idle habits (polish,
  patrol, phone, lean on the rail, sit on a table), the double-take, walk, rush or march, the
  wind-up, the attack (shove, rush, fling, throw), the skid, hurt, knocked down, dazed (sitting or
  lying, stars), getting up, walking off, slipping (feet out, flat), frozen, sulking, ducking,
  hopping back, sidestepping, running off.
- **Procedural layers**: breathing, leaning into speed, squash on landing, the head's follow-through,
  and a limp layer for tumbles: while flying, each limb swings loose on a damped spring toward
  hanging under gravity, so a knocked body flops rather than spinning stiff; it bounces once and
  slides.
- **Effects** (particles, sprites, comic words): the ! burst, stars, glass shards and ice, the water
  spill, coffee splashes and puddles, the hat, paper planes and their confetti, slime on a snotted
  staffer, the chain's points, OUT OF ORDER, the projector beam and his silhouette on the screen.
- **New sounds** in the shipped `AudioBus`'s procedural style: the ! sting, cart wheels squealing and
  rolling, glass shattering, a splash, the paper whoosh, the phone chime, the order-up ding. The
  existing `kick`, `slip`, `stomp`, `bonk`, `thud` and `whistle` are reused.
- **Speech bubbles**: rounded boxes with a tail, placed by the face-safe layout.

## 8. The face rule for the cast

Nothing of the cast covers Hammer's face: the cast, their props and their projectiles always draw
behind Hammer; planes, carts and coffee stop at his collider; particles that would reach his face
box are dropped (the existing rule); speech bubbles, ! bursts, stars and points go through the
face-safe layout.

## 9. Also in R2b-1: R2a's deferred minors

1. Going through a door just after crouching leaves his collider crouch-height until his next jump:
   `enter()` in `game.ts` sets `h.h = HERO_H`.
2. Pickups and punch clocks no longer count during the lethal tumble before he lies still.
3. A swing that lands during a crate's 0.16 s bump lock is not spent on it (`hitCrate` reports
   whether it acted).
4. The camera zoom snaps (no easing) across a door into or out of the stash (`showLevel`).
5. The mezzanine hint's "↓ + jump drops you back down" shows only on the balcony, not the stairs;
   `MEZZANINE` and `EARLY_X` in `src/render/hud.ts` come from level data.
6. The pickup anchor at (11026, −70) over the escalator's lowest steps is removed.
7. The start card no longer covers the touch pad.
8. The door glow and the door hint go off through dying and game over.
9. `togglePause` sends a `mode` event.

## 10. Phases

- **R2b-1, the cast plays**: the cast in the sim (bodies, the four roles' behavior, the director,
  planes, coffee and carts as threats, contact and damage, what Hammer's moves do to them, puddles,
  the chain, the shaft, knockouts and walking off, points, lines and recordings), the placement in
  the lobby data, coming back after a lost life, the scripted player against the live cast, the
  stand-in figures with every clip, the effects and sounds, the new hints, and the nine minors.
  Ends playable, with boards.
- **R2b-2, the painted cast**: the generalized cut and rig, the eight people and the hat cut,
  registered and skinned, head scales, recolors, the face edits if delivered, rim light. Starts
  when the paintings are in. Ends with the same boards painted.

Each half gets its own plan (writing-plans), executed inline with TDD, and one fresh reviewer at
the end.

## 11. Testing

- **Sim checks** (`scripts/cast.check.mjs`, pure functions only): each role notices, winds up for at
  least its time before anything can hurt, and hurts only while its attack is under way; a plain
  bump costs no pip; never more than two tickets out; mallet, snot, stomp, lecture, burst and shield
  do what §3.2 says to each role; the bellhop's two hits and the slide; a bonked cart reverses and
  bowls; the chain's points and the life at eight; puddles trip staff and never Hammer; students
  duck, sidestep and hop back; planes fly a deterministic glide, can be swatted and are capped at
  three; the shaft; knocked-out cast never hurts; the cast resets on a lost life and survives a stash
  trip unchanged; determinism with the cast on.
- **The scripted player** (`src/sim/drive.ts`) clears both routes with the cast live: it jumps
  carts, bonks staff, swats planes and lectures students.
- **Face**: the cast draws behind Hammer, projectiles stop at his collider, and the face-safe layout
  covers speech bubbles, bursts, stars and points (a placement sweep, as for his thoughts).
- **The existing checks** (`test:next`, `test:game`, every browser check) keep passing; the older
  browser checks that test unrelated things calm the cast with a dev helper.
- **A browser check** (`scripts/cast-browser.check.mjs`) plays the fights: the first bellhop's
  shove, a cart jumped and one bonked back, a server's rush and knockdown, a lecture freezing staff
  and schooling a student, a plane swatted, the shield bowling.
- **Boards** (`scripts/look-boards.mjs`): `cast.png` (each person in every clip, eight frames each),
  `cast-lobby-1..6.png` (the lobby with the cast at six spots), `cast-fx.png` (the effects), each
  checked at full size and stepped frame by frame.
